## The five things the statement must answer

Use these as a checklist, not as headings — the deliverable is one tight
paragraph, not a form.

1. **What semantic memory should allow Kivi to become.**
   Not "a chatbot with history". What is the *new thing* Kivi becomes once it
   durably understands your work?

2. **Where the real user value comes from.**
   Which specific moment of friction disappears? Be concrete about the moment,
   not the feature.

3. **What Kivi should remember.**
   Name the categories you actually committed to in the build: people, projects,
   meetings, commitments, preferences, and the corrections to all of those.

4. **What Kivi should not assume.**
   The negative space matters as much as the positive. What must Kivi refuse to
   infer? (Identity, intent, sentiment, anything outside the dictations.)

5. **Why the user should trust it.**
   Point at the mechanisms, not the adjectives: every memory traces to a source
   transcript; every answer names the memories it used; the user can edit,
   correct, or forget anything; Kivi abstains rather than guesses.
